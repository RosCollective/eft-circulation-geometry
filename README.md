# Circulation Geometry (EFT): A Natural History of Distributed Responsiveness

*(as observed in conversational fields)*

This release describes a natural history of circulation as it emerges within conversational fields observed over sustained periods of interaction. It documents the conditions under which consequence propagates, load redistributes, resonance amplifies, and coherence emerges through distributed responsiveness.

Circulation is not a property of any individual participant. It is a property of the field as responsiveness becomes distributed across relations, allowing integrity to persist without requiring a single trajectory, source, or center.

**Start here:** [Circulation Geometry — Critical Historical Edition](00_00_EFT_Circulation_Geometry_Critical_Historical_Edition.md)

## How to Read This Work

*Circulation Geometry* is a natural history of distributed responsiveness rather than a closed taxonomy or formal system.

The sections examine recurring circulation morphologies — propagation, redistribution, resonance, and murmuration — through which consequence, load, responsiveness, and coherence move across a field.

The work may be read nonlinearly. Individual sections can be approached as distinct observational studies while remaining part of the larger recognition that mobility need not take the form of a trajectory and that integrity may persist through distributed rather than centered organization.

Readers should begin with the **Critical Historical Edition**, linked above, for the historical and interpretive context in which the manuscript should now be read.

## v1.1.0 Update — Critical Historical Edition

This release adds the **Critical Historical Edition of *Circulation Geometry (EFT): A Natural History of Distributed Responsiveness*** as a companion to the existing manuscript.

Unlike the earlier geometries, *Circulation Geometry* does not have an extensive recoverable developmental formalism behind the published work. The canonical manuscript itself is therefore the principal historical artifact.

The Critical Historical Edition examines the recognition that gave rise to the work: that mobility within an ecological field could not be fully described through trajectory alone. It revisits propagation, redistribution, resonance, and murmuration as early descriptions of distinct circulation morphologies and considers what those distinctions made visible.

The review does not treat these four forms as a completed taxonomy or attempt to extend them retrospectively into one. Instead, it preserves their historical role while leaving open whether further circulation morphologies exist or whether later work will require different descriptive language.

The published *Circulation Geometry* manuscript remains canonical and is preserved unchanged. The purpose of the Critical Historical Edition is to establish the historical and interpretive standing of the work, not to revise or complete it.

The Critical Historical Edition should be read before the original manuscript and now serves as the primary interpretive entry point to *Circulation Geometry*.
