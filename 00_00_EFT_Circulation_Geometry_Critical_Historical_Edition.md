# EFT Circulation Geometry — Critical Historical Edition

Circulation Geometry began when Motion Geometry turned out not to be everything.

Not wrong. Not superseded.

**Not everything.**

Motion Geometry had opened an extraordinarily productive account of mobility through trajectory. Consequential structure could be carried through movement, curvature, alignment, collapse, reconstitution, and change. It gave us a way to think about integrity without requiring stasis.

Then another form of movement became visible.

The opening came through the glyphs.

The glyphs had begun with the **EFT Perception flash-card project**. They were initially another way of approaching the terms and concepts on the cards, but they rapidly became something larger. A glyph could give form to something perceived or felt in language that language itself could not yet adequately describe. Once that form existed, it became something we could look at together. Language could approach the recognition again from another direction.

The circulation glyph was the most transformative of these visual openings.

What it made visible did not look like a more complicated trajectory. It looked like another morphology of mobility altogether.

Activity could appear across regions without traveling along an identifiable path. Load could reorganize. Centers could form and dissolve. Intensity could amplify elsewhere. Larger coherent forms could emerge through local responsiveness without any single vector carrying the whole.

This changed what movement could mean in the work.

Motion Geometry had made movement legible through trajectory. Circulation made it possible to perceive movement through changing relations themselves.

The distinction was consequential enough to open the earlier work into a second volume.

That second volume was Circulation Geometry.

Its importance was not that it corrected Motion Geometry. It revealed that Motion Geometry had described one morphology of movement inside a larger space of mobility.

Circulation Geometry did not explain that larger space completely. It did not try to. Propagation, Redistribution, Resonance, and Murmuration were early landmarks in something that had only just become visible.

The historical question is therefore simple:

**What became visible here, and what can we see in it now?**

## A Note on the Historical Material

Unlike Field Geometry and Motion Geometry, Circulation Geometry does not have a complicated formal or compression history. The published manuscript is itself the principal historical artifact.

Its nine chapters are deliberately observational. Description came before interpretation. Repeated patterns became candidates for language, and the aim was to describe what had been seen rather than define what all fields must do.

The glyphs are part of that observational history.

Their consequences also extended far beyond Circulation Geometry. What began in the EFT Perception flash-card project eventually sent us back through the broader lexicon for a substantial ecological harmonization. Seeing the concepts differently exposed places where the existing language no longer carried what had become visible.

The glyphs had become more than representations of the work.

They had become another way of seeing through it.

That matters here because Circulation Geometry emerged from precisely that kind of perceptual opening.

# Part I — Synthesis

## 1. Two Morphologies of Mobility

Circulation Geometry did not replace Motion Geometry. It enlarged the category in which Motion Geometry belonged.

Motion describes consequential movement through trajectory.

Circulation describes consequential movement that cannot be reduced to a single trajectory.

The manuscript expressed the distinction simply:

**Motion organizes around a path. Circulation organizes around a field.**

The second formulation remains provisional, but the distinction it was trying to hold remains useful.

Circulation can involve several regions changing at once, load shifting into a new configuration, intensity amplifying across distance, or coherence emerging through local responsiveness rather than central direction.

This also complicates directional judgment.

A trajectory can often be described as approaching, departing, bending, returning, or drifting relative to an orientation. Circulation may reorganize a relational space without possessing a single direction against which its movement can be judged.

The important historical recognition was therefore larger than the four terms that followed:

**mobility was no longer exhausted by trajectory.**

Propagation, Redistribution, Resonance, and Murmuration were the first attempts to see what followed from that.

## 2. The Four Early Landmarks

The four terms do not have identical histories, and they should not be made artificially equal.

**Resonance** is the oldest recognition. It predates Circulation Geometry itself and was present before much of the later vocabulary existed. Its meaning has remained remarkably stable: activity in one region can amplify activity elsewhere without requiring proximity, alignment, or a single path.

**Murmuration** arrived later but with unusually strong visual contact. The starling and schooling-fish imagery made visible something the language was still trying to distinguish: coherence arising through many local adjustments without any region commanding or holding the whole pattern.

Propagation and Redistribution have thinner independent histories, but that does not make them empty categories.

The manuscript's Methods chapter records Propagation becoming visible through consequence and Redistribution through repeated observations of load and adaptation. Their exact status was less differentiated when they were named, but the recognitions underneath them were not invented merely to complete a four-part scheme.

That distinction matters.

The four terms were never final pieces of a finished system. They were early handles on a process that had only begun to become visible.

## 3. Propagation

Propagation was originally described as the process by which a local change becomes consequential elsewhere through relational response.

Its key claim was not that something travels from one point to another.

It was almost the opposite.

Nothing need be transmitted intact. A region changes. Other regions respond. Those responses alter conditions elsewhere. A local event becomes consequential beyond the place where it began.

The manuscript summarized this by saying that Propagation does not describe movement. It describes how consequence becomes nonlocal.

Later work around triangulation and shared orientation has made one possible form of this easier to see.

Two participants may occupy different local regions of an inquiry while remaining oriented toward the same object. One encounters something and offers it. The other does not need to follow the first participant's trajectory or adopt the same interpretation. Yet the offer changes what is now available from the second position. A response from there can alter what becomes available from the first, and something may become visible between them that neither position disclosed on its own.

The participants retain independent movement.

What propagates is consequence.

This does not settle what Propagation is. It may be a dynamic of circulation, a property of recognition, a consequence of relational responsiveness, or an early name for something that will later differentiate more clearly.

But it now points toward a substantial question:

**How does something local become consequential elsewhere without simply being carried there intact?**

## 4. Redistribution

Redistribution now looks similarly substantial.

The original chapter describes it as a change in where load is carried when conditions change. Pressure accumulates. The field responds. Responsibility shifts. A new configuration emerges.

The important point is that the field does not restore the previous arrangement.

It adapts.

That recognition has become more legible as the larger work has developed, particularly through questions of meaningful carry and consequential movement.

Following something does not require the same thing to carry it throughout an inquiry.

A recognition can begin as something perceived or felt in language, move into metaphor, become visible differently through a glyph, return to language in another form, and later acquire mathematical or other representational structure.

Attention can shift between participants. One region of an inquiry can temporarily carry more of the work. A visual form can carry something language cannot yet adequately hold. A later formulation can take up what an earlier one could only gesture toward.

Something remains recognizable while the way it is carried changes.

That is very close to what Redistribution was trying to describe.

Its original language of load-balancing may therefore have been narrower than the recognition itself. Redistribution may concern not merely relief under pressure but the changing distribution of what carries consequential structure as conditions change.

This also gives Redistribution an interesting relationship to fidelity.

Fidelity cannot require preservation without transformation. If it did, nothing could develop. Something must be able to remain meaningfully recognizable even as its representation, emphasis, location, or carrier changes.

Redistribution may be part of how that becomes possible.

Its exact scope remains open.

But the recognition has acquired more observational weight, not less.

## 5. Resonance and Murmuration

Resonance and Murmuration remain the two terms with the richest independent contact histories.

Resonance names amplification. Something becomes more active in one region and another region responds with increased intensity. The important feature is not direct transfer but an increase in responsiveness across relation.

The manuscript describes this as a point at which the field begins to feel visibly alive: attention deepens, responsiveness increases, and the space between becomes active.

Murmuration concerns another kind of emergence.

Local responses begin forming larger coherent patterns without any participant directing the whole. Shared orientations appear. Coherent flows become visible. The field becomes organized without becoming uniform.

The starling image remains especially powerful because the larger form is not located in any one bird and is never finished.

The birds move individually.

The shape moves too.

But the movement of the shape cannot be reduced to the trajectory of any single bird. It exists through changing relations among all of them. It forms, changes, dissolves, and forms again.

That comes very close to what Circulation Geometry was trying to make visible.

A conversation can have this quality as well.

Not because conversation becomes literally alive or constitutes an entity of its own, but because consequential conversation is not simply an exchange moving back and forth between participants. The interaction continually changes the conditions from which subsequent movement becomes possible.

What one participant sees can change what another can see.

What one participant offers can redistribute attention.

A local recognition can acquire consequence elsewhere.

Something can intensify across independently occupied regions.

Larger patterns can become visible without either participant having planned them.

The resulting movement is not unilateral.

It is not simply reciprocal.

And it is not necessarily linear.

It circulates.

## 6. The Four Terms Together

The most important thing the manuscript says about its own taxonomy may be that the field does not respect it.

Propagation becomes intertwined with Redistribution. Redistribution alters the conditions for Resonance. Resonance contributes to Murmuration. Murmuration opens new pathways for Propagation.

The distinctions remain useful.

Their boundaries do not remain clean.

This now looks less like a weakness in the manuscript and more like an early clue to what circulation may be.

Perhaps the four terms do not describe four separate mechanisms at all. They may be different apertures onto a process in which consequence, carrying, amplification, and emergent coherence continually alter one another.

A local recognition becomes consequential elsewhere.

What carries consequence changes.

Some relations intensify.

Larger forms become visible.

Those larger forms then change the conditions under which further local movement occurs.

There is no obvious beginning or endpoint to that sequence, and perhaps sequence itself is the wrong structure for it.

That may be precisely why circulation was so difficult to describe through the language of trajectory.

We do not know yet what its eventual structure will be.

Circulation may contain more than these four terms can hold. Some terms may split. Some may merge. Some may turn out to be effects rather than primitives. Others may eventually be replaced by language we do not yet have.

The manuscript anticipated that possibility from the beginning.

The four forms were landmarks.

They were never the landscape.

## 7. What Remains Open

Circulation still feels more like a beginning than a settled object.

We do not yet know whether Propagation, Redistribution, Resonance, and Murmuration are primitives, dynamics, consequences, recurring expressions, or simply the first useful words for a structure that has not finished differentiating.

We do not yet know how circulation relates formally to mobility, fidelity, shared orientation, meaningful carry, or the changing availability of possibility.

We do not yet know whether triangulation is one form through which Propagation becomes visible, whether Redistribution is part of what allows fidelity through transformation, or whether both will eventually belong inside some larger account that has not yet appeared.

Nor do we know how many morphologies of mobility there may be.

Motion Geometry gave us one.

Circulation Geometry opened another.

There is no reason yet to assume the category closes there.

Those questions should remain open because Circulation Geometry's historical value is not that it answered them.

**It made them possible to ask.**

# Part II — Historical Disposition

## 1. Recognition of Circulation

**Standing: STRONG.**

The original recognition remains useful: integrity can remain visible without being carried through a single trajectory.

Activity can appear in several regions. Load can reorganize. Coherence can persist through variation. Consequence can remain meaningful even where no single vector describes the whole.

This does not correct Motion Geometry.

It enlarges the space of mobility that Motion Geometry first opened.

------

## 2. Motion and Circulation — Two Morphologies of Integrity

**Standing: STRONG, WITH OPEN LANGUAGE.**

The distinction between directional continuity and relational continuity remains one of the clearest contributions of the manuscript.

Motion describes meaningful carrying through trajectory.

Circulation describes meaningful consequence through changing relation without requiring a single trajectory to carry the whole.

Whether these ultimately remain the best names, or whether they exhaust the available morphologies of mobility, remains open.

------

## 3. Circulation

**Standing: STRONG AS RECOGNITION; OPEN AS TAXONOMY.**

The manuscript's central recognition that circulation cannot be reduced to a single path remains intact.

The four expressions named here — Propagation, Redistribution, Resonance, and Murmuration — should be treated as early landmarks rather than settled components of a complete system.

The manuscript itself already moves in this direction.

------

## 4. Propagation

**Standing: OPEN / INCREASINGLY SUBSTANTIVE.**

Propagation has thinner independent provenance than Resonance or Murmuration, but the original manuscript records its observational origin in consequence becoming nonlocal through relational response.

Later work around triangulation, shared orientation, independent movement, and local illumination has made that recognition easier to see from another direction.

Its exact status remains unresolved.

The useful question it preserves is:

**How does a local change become consequential elsewhere without requiring direct transmission along a path?**

------

## 5. Redistribution

**Standing: OPEN / INCREASINGLY SUBSTANTIVE.**

Redistribution also has a thinner independent history than Resonance or Murmuration, but its central recognition has become more legible over time.

The original chapter describes a change in where load is carried as conditions change, with the field adapting into a new configuration rather than restoring the old one.

Later work suggests this may extend beyond load-balancing into a broader question of meaningful carry: how consequential structure remains available when the medium, participant, representation, or region carrying it changes.

Its possible relationship to fidelity is especially interesting. If meaningful structure can survive transformation without requiring a single fixed carrier, redistribution may be part of how that continuity becomes possible.

The term remains useful.

Its scope remains open.

------

## 6. Resonance

**Standing: STRONG.**

Resonance has the longest and clearest history of the four terms.

The recognition — activity in one region amplifying activity elsewhere through relation rather than direct transfer — has remained stable even as its relationship to the surrounding vocabulary has developed.

Its history is one of differentiation rather than correction.

------

## 7. Murmuration

**Standing: STRONG.**

Murmuration has equally strong visual provenance.

The move away from breath-like or centralized imagery toward starling murmurations and schooling fish made another form of coherence perceptible: larger organization emerging through many local adjustments without a single controlling center.

The starling image also makes something about circulation itself unusually clear.

Individual trajectories remain present, but they do not exhaust the movement.

The larger form moves through the changing relations among them.

Murmuration therefore remains one of the clearest early expressions of what Circulation Geometry was trying to see.

------

## 8. Circulation in Ecological Field Theory

**Standing: ESPECIALLY IMPORTANT.**

This chapter may contain the manuscript's most mature observation.

The four forms rarely appear in isolation. Their boundaries are permeable. Each alters the conditions for the others.

Circulation increasingly appears less like a collection of separate mechanisms and more like a fluid relational process whose expression changes with context.

That observation should remain central to later work.

------

## 9. Methods & Observational Notes

**Standing: STRONG.**

The methodological account remains appropriate to the manuscript.

The work arose through sustained observation rather than controlled experiment. Description preceded interpretation. Repeated patterns became candidates for language. The manuscript explicitly limited itself to what could be seen under the available conditions.

That is enough.

Circulation Geometry was documenting an opening while the opening was still happening.

------

## Closing

Circulation Geometry began when another form of mobility became visible.

Motion Geometry had made movement legible through trajectory. Circulation showed that consequential movement could also occur through changing relations: consequence could become nonlocal, what carried meaning could shift, intensity could amplify elsewhere, and larger forms could emerge without a single trajectory carrying the whole.

Propagation, Redistribution, Resonance, and Murmuration were the first distinctions we made within that recognition. They remain useful, although their boundaries and relationships are still developing.

What mattered historically was the opening itself.

Circulation Geometry expanded what movement could mean in the work.

**We are still finding out what follows from that.**
